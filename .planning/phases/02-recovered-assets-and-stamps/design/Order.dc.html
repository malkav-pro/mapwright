The two behave differently on purpose. Scaling resizes each stamp about its own anchor — a forest gets bigger trees, not a spread-out forest. Rotating turns the whole arrangement about one shared centre — the proposed convention is the bounding-box centre of the unlocked selection, computed once when the drag begins so the pivot cannot drift mid-gesture. Each stamp also turns by the same angle, so the group reads as rigid.

This is a temporary selection behaviour, not a group object: deselecting leaves no persistent grouping. A completed drag is one undo step covering every affected stamp; Esc mid-drag cancels the whole preview.

The acceptance fixture is an unbatched draw-order reference, and the third figure is what failing it looks like — the same three stamps, a different picture where they overlap.

Picking

A normal click takes the topmost visible alpha-aware hit: the nearest-to-front object whose actual pixels are opaque enough under the pointer. Transparent padding never intercepts a click as if it were artwork. Alt-click steps down the stack of hits at that point, showing the position in the stack each time and wrapping at the bottom. An object obscured completely is still reachable from the order list, which is also the keyboard route.

Mixed is never rendered as 0 or as a blank field — either would read as a real value. Typing an absolute number into Size sets all three to it. Scale by is the relative control and keeps the variation. Rotation and opacity offer the same pair; position is absolute only for one stamp and relative (Move by) for several.

One of the four you dragged over is locked

"3 of 4 selected. ridge-peak-c is on a locked layer and was left out — it is excluded from the shared rotation centre too." The marquee does not silently skip it and does not silently unlock it: the count says three, the row shows a padlock, and Unlock layer is the explicit next action. A hidden layer behaves the same way, with Show layer.

Duplicate and paste land just in front of the frontmost source row, offset by a small fixed amount, selected and ready to drag. Copies get new entity identities and keep the same asset references — a duplicate is a second placement of one asset, never a second copy of the bytes. Paste is same-map only in P0.
