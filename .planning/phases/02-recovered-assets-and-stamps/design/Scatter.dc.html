Scatter brush · Shift+S
Painting — a preview, nothing committed

Drag across the map and the brush lays stamps from the chosen set as you go. Everything drawn during the drag is a preview: Esc discards the whole stroke, and nothing has entered history. Release resolves the stroke into ordinary stamps and commits one undoable operation — not 142.

Preview, committed, regenerated — three different things
Preview

During the drag. Drawn at 50%, not in the order list, not in history, never in an export. Esc discards it; releasing the pointer outside the canvas still ends the stroke normally.

Committed

One command holding 142 resolved placements — each with its asset, position, scale and angle written down. They are now ordinary stamps: individually selectable, movable, deletable, reorderable.

Regenerated

A deliberate, separately labelled action: Re-roll on a committed group discards its placements and resolves new ones from a new seed. Its own undo step. Nothing else in Mapwright does this.

Changing the controls in this panel afterwards
A future Mapwright with a different algorithm

The resolved placements are the stored truth, so none of the above re-runs anything. The seed and RNG identity are kept beside them so a draw can be reproduced or explained — not so it has to be recomputed. A seed alone would not survive an algorithm change; this does.

Spacing is an average, not a rule

51 map units is the mean distance between neighbouring stamp centres, with natural variation around it. Two trees will sometimes sit closer and overlap, which is what a forest looks like. Nothing here is a minimum-distance solver, and nothing reads the terrain underneath: the brush does not avoid the sea, the river or a road, and does not claim to.

Spacing is in map units and does not change with zoom, and it is not a percentage of stamp size — raising the size range does not spread the trees out.

Random flip, tint and opacity

Not offered here. They were considered and left out of this scatter, so there is no control to find and no disabled row pretending there will be one. All three remain available per stamp once the group is committed.

used for the next stroke

A new seed changes what the next stroke draws. It does not disturb any stroke already committed. To change one of those, select its group and use Re-roll, which is a separate, separately undoable command.
